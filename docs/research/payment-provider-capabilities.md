# Payment provider capabilities

Research date: **2026-10-24**. Decision-support research for [issue #6](https://github.com/jeffnawroth/source-taster/issues/6), under [map #4](https://github.com/jeffnawroth/source-taster/issues/4). Baseline: the adopted English requirements specification v3.1 unchanged and root GLOSSARY.md. The legacy project is out of scope and was not consulted.

## Scope and evidence method

Compare direct merchant processing (Stripe, Mollie) with merchant of record (Paddle, FastSpring) for monthly Pro and one-time expiring Check packs. FA-KTO-20–28 and **launch preparation FA-ERW-06** govern the comparison; FA-KTO-25/29/33/34 are deferred functionality whose preparation still matters. A PDF invoice is not proof of an EN 16931 structured invoice. Public vendor documentation, API schemas, contracts and official law/tax guidance are evidence; vendor approval, executed contracts, tax/legal review and acceptance tests remain separate gates. No provider, billing/quota model or requirements change is approved by this research.

Evidence labels used below: **V** = documented vendor capability, **I** = integration or Source Taster implementation required, **U** = not established from the inspected primary sources, **G** = a gate preventing an unconditional recommendation. “U” does not assert that a vendor cannot provide a feature.

## Findings that govern the shortlist

**Assessment:** no candidate can yet be declared to satisfy the entire scope without conditions. The strongest direct-merchant investigation paths are **Stripe Payments + Billing + Invoicing + a named e-invoicing integration**, and **Mollie Payments + Subscriptions + Sales Invoices**. Stripe explicitly states that compliant structured e-invoice generation/transmission is external; Mollie documents native structured XML delivery for DE/NL/BE but its inspected schema only proves a PDF memo for a customer PO. Neither observation alone proves FA-ERW-06 complete. [S4][M3][M4]

**Paddle Billing** is the clearest published-price MoR comparator: invoice terms, bank transfer and a dedicated PO field are documented, as are card and PayPal purchases/subscriptions. **FastSpring** is a credible quote-based MoR alternative with invoice/PO workflows and future SEPA. However, the inspected invoice documentation of neither establishes EN 16931/XRechnung support. **This is an unsatisfied evidence gate for FA-ERW-06, not a minor future enhancement.** A foreign MoR seller potentially changes statutory domestic B2B invoicing applicability; it does not waive the specification's explicit preparation requirement. [P1][P2][P3][F1][F2][F3][L5]

No provider manages a Check pack's remaining Quota, 30-day expiry or Pro-feature access merely because it accepts a one-time payment. Those are Source Taster responsibilities, with immutable purchase terms and payment reconciliation; the billing/Quota model remains a downstream decision. The requirements themselves define these product semantics (FA-KTO-10/15/20, FA-ERW-01); provider transaction/subscription APIs provide payment primitives, not that domain model. [S1][M1][P2][F4]

## Seller, VAT and customer-country evidence

### Direct merchant is not merchant of record

**Verified:** Stripe's direct Payments/Billing contract is a payment-services arrangement; Mollie's agreement expressly leaves Mollie outside the merchant/customer sales contract. Source Taster's operator remains the seller, responsible for product terms, correct tax treatment, registrations/returns, invoice accuracy, consumer obligations and support. Buying Stripe Tax or selecting Mollie's `vatScheme: one-stop-shop` does not make the provider the reseller or automatically register/file the operator's OSS return. Stripe offers separately priced tax/registration/filing services, which require their own scope; the baseline price below includes only calculation. [S8][S12][S13][M4][M9]

**Verified MoR distinction:** Paddle's supplier terms appoint Paddle a non-exclusive reseller and allocate calculation/collection/remittance of Sales Taxes to Paddle using the supplier's Product Information; the supplier indemnifies inaccurate/omitted information. Its **buyer** terms identify Paddle.com Inc. for US buyers, Paddle.com (Canada) Ltd. for Canada, and **Paddle.com Market Limited (England and Wales) for the rest of the world**, including German buyers. Do not confuse the operator's Paddle supplier-contract entity (which may be Paddle Payments Limited in Ireland) with the legal seller to a German purchaser. Confirm the executed terms and actual invoice seller. [P4][P5]

FastSpring's buyer terms describe resale by the FastSpring entities collectively: **Bright Market, LLC d/b/a FastSpring (US), FastSpring BV (Netherlands), FastSpring Limited (UK), and SalesRight Technologies ULC d/b/a FastSpring (Canada)**. The inspected buyer terms do **not** establish a deterministic German-buyer seller routing rule. Obtain the actual order/invoice entity and regional addendum rather than simply calling every EU sale “FastSpring Netherlands.” Its tax documentation says FastSpring calculates, files and remits taxes for platform sales. Vendor terms still require product registration, accurate onboarding, approval and the applicable order form. [F6][F7][F9]

**Assessment:** for covered MoR resales, the MoR handles buyer-facing indirect tax; the operator still needs advice on its supply to/reseller payouts from the MoR, domestic bookkeeping, business/income taxes, correct product classification and transactions sold outside that MoR. Neither “all taxes handled” marketing nor a MoR payout makes those duties disappear. Paddle explicitly ties tax treatment to accurate Product Information; FastSpring's representation is scoped to sales through its platform. Refund/chargeback/reserve/payout terms and migration/export ability must be accepted before a low-operations recommendation becomes a procurement decision. [P4][P5][F6][F9]

### FA-KTO-34: €8,000 is a product trigger, not the legal threshold

**Verified official rule:** the Commission describes a **€10,000 excluding-VAT aggregate** threshold for qualifying cross-border B2C telecommunications/broadcasting/electronic services and intra-Community distance sales, conditional on establishment in only one Member State and not exceeding the threshold in the **current or preceding calendar year**. Above it, destination rules apply from the transaction that exceeds it; opting for destination taxation below the threshold binds the supplier for two calendar years. OSS is a simplification for declaring covered VAT, not an exemption from domestic VAT returns. [L6]

**Implication for the unchanged specification:** section 19's **€8,000 calendar-year review trigger** is an early-warning point for reassessment, not authorization to use German VAT whenever current-year Source Taster receipts are below €10,000. The legal test may include the operator's other qualifying sales and preceding year; tax classification, exemption/Kleinunternehmer status, voluntary elections, returns/refunds and seller establishment must be assessed by the tax advisor under FA-KTO-24. FA-EIN-03's monthly review should flag the approaching threshold early enough to enable destination taxation in time. Do not defer a legal obligation until the next specification meeting. [L6; project FA-KTO-24/34, FA-EIN-02/03]

Section 19 explicitly says FA-KTO-34 is not needed if the provider acts as MoR **and handles VAT**. Apply that exception only to covered MoR buyer sales; it does not automatically cover a later parallel direct-invoice organization channel. With a foreign reseller, destination VAT may apply from the first covered resale; forcing the operator's below-threshold German VAT logic into the MoR checkout would be the wrong seller model. This is a candidate clarification of FA-KTO-24, not an approved rewrite. [L6][P4][P5][F6]

### Country and tax evidence are separate from a UI country dropdown

**Verified:** Stripe Tax chooses a customer location from shipping/billing/payment-method/IP data according to the documented integration hierarchy; its API path requires a supplied valid address and does not silently use the Checkout hierarchy. Mollie Sales Invoices requires recipient country and offers VAT number and inclusive/exclusive modes. Paddle transactions associate customer, address and business records; FastSpring says billing country/postal code are required for tax determination. None of those facts, alone, establishes that Source Taster will retain all evidence required for its selected tax treatment. [S8][M4][P2][F6]

**Verified legal evidence rule:** Implementing Regulation 282/2011, Articles 24b(d) and 24f (consolidation 2025-04-14, read through EUR-Lex in the browser), describes the ordinary presumption based on **two non-contradictory evidence items**. Article 24f includes billing address, IP/geolocation, bank/payment-country information, SIM/fixed-line information and other commercially relevant information. Article 24b also provides a **different €100,000 excluding-VAT current-and-preceding-year threshold** for a conditional one-item simplification: that item must come from another person involved in the supply, rather than the supplier/customer, and fall under Article 24f(a)–(e). This is not the €10,000 place-of-supply threshold and does not mean a customer-selected country is always sufficient. Article 63c requires detailed OSS records including tax amounts, payments and the evidence used to determine customer location, available electronically without delay. Applicability and the latest legally effective amendments require advisor confirmation. [L7]

**Design recommendation / tax confirmation needed:** persist a purchase-time snapshot of seller, customer country, address evidence, tax status/VAT-ID validation result and time, currency, taxable amount, rate/tax amount, tax jurisdiction, product tax category and provider transaction/invoice IDs. Ask the advisor which evidence rule/presumption applies to this service and how conflicting billing, IP and bank-country evidence is resolved; then verify the selected integration exposes and retains those evidence items. Collect only necessary data under that approved policy. A vendor location hierarchy or a successful tax calculation does not itself prove the legal evidence set is retained/exportable. [S8][L7]

**Verified retention distinction:** the Commission says OSS records must be retained **10 years from the end of the transaction year**, even after leaving the scheme. This may exceed FA-KTO-04's eight-year billing-record wording when the operator uses OSS; fiscal invoice storage and OSS evidence are not automatically the same record class. Ask the advisor/client to resolve the applicable retention schedule before that channel starts; MoR-controlled buyer records and operator-retained supplier records must be scoped separately. [L8]

For FA-BTR-06/FA-KTO-34, the reporting design should reconcile gross/net/VAT/refunds by country and period to provider payouts, preserve audit links and export the actual required OSS fields. A payout-country total is not customer-country evidence. For MoR resale records, label the legal seller and separate buyer sales/tax from operator supplier proceeds to prevent an accountant treating both as the same turnover ledger. These are proposed accounting integration controls; tax-advisor sign-off remains required. [P4][F6][L6]

## German consumer purchase, consent and cancellation

**Verified law, not a legal opinion:** BGB §312j requires the payment obligation to be explicit on the final order button; §312k requires the cancellation button, direct confirmation page, permanently/easily accessible route, durable declaration and immediate electronic text confirmation. §309 no. 9 limits automatic extension/notice terms for applicable standard consumer contracts. General portal availability or a subscription API does not prove those customer-facing obligations. [L1][L3][L4]

**Proposed Source Taster implementation boundary:** expose “Verträge hier kündigen” on every relevant public/product page, including a route usable without an existing account login or a newly obtained portal magic link. Let the consumer identify the contract, state ordinary/extraordinary cancellation and desired date, and submit on the direct “jetzt kündigen” page without offers. Record the declaration, receipt time, requested/effective date and delivery address before asynchronous provider work; send immediate text confirmation, provide a durable copy, prevent further billing as instructed, retain paid access until its proper end and surface failed synchronization to the operator. Identity verification must not become an extra sign-in obstacle to sending a legally effective declaration; legal review determines adequate identification and abuse protection. **Custom API cancellation is feasible; whether a MoR authorizes Source Taster to receive/carry out declarations for its buyer contract needs written agreement.** [L3][S9][M6][P3][F8]

Hosted portals remain useful for ordinary billing: Stripe supports configurable plan-change/cancellation flows, Paddle's portal uses an email magic link and provides invoices/cancellation/payment updates, and FastSpring's portal provides subscription/payment management. They must be integrated into Source Taster's account rather than mistaken for its entire account UI. In particular, Paddle's documented portal does not establish an arbitrary product upgrade/downgrade UI; build the chosen change flow through its API. Mollie documents APIs and an operator Web App; no general native customer subscription portal was established here. [S7][P3][F8][M1]

**FA-KTO-23 is a legal classification gate.** BGB §356 distinguishes services (withdrawal generally ends on full performance subject to conditions) from paid digital content supplied without a tangible medium (different express-consent/acknowledgment/confirmation conditions). An ongoing monthly checking service cannot simply be assumed to lose withdrawal rights as soon as a checkbox is clicked. Have counsel classify Pro and Check packs and approve immediate-start/withdrawal wording and consequences separately. Also verify current online withdrawal-function duties for the eventual launch date; withdrawal and terminating future renewal are distinct actions. No silent requirement change or waiver is made here. [L2; project FA-UEB-23]

**Proposed evidence/activation flow:** retain exact German/English terms and consent text versions, separate unticked choices, authenticated purchase/owner ID, timestamp and checkout correlation; confirm the consent plus withdrawal instructions by durable email, then apply the approved start rule. If consent is absent, define the deferred-start purchase or blocked-immediate-start behavior rather than granting paid service through a success redirect. A standard “accept terms” checkbox is not equivalent to the separate FA-KTO-23 consent. Stripe's general checkout customization, a MoR's standard buyer terms and a receipt email do not prove the required wording or evidence export. [S6][P5][F7][L2]

**Notifications:** FA-KTO-50/FA-UEB-01 still require German/English purchase-with-invoice, failed-payment and cancellation emails. Paddle sends compliance/lifecycle emails and its sandbox documents delivery limitations; Mollie recommends communicating recurring charges. Define which sender owns each notice, trigger, locale, required wording and evidence; ensure duplicate webhooks do not duplicate email or activate service prematurely. Provider receipts may complement Source Taster consent/cancellation messages, but do not establish that all content has been sent. Annual renewal/FA-KTO-51 remains deferred; retain a hook for notices and an annual-to-indefinite transition if FA-KTO-32 is later adopted. [P7][M1][S7][F8; project FA-KTO-28/50/51]

**SCA acceptance:** Stripe documents issuer/exemption behavior and optional 3DS requests; Paddle documents 3DS2 and a challenged test card; FastSpring documents its PSD2/SCA handling; Mollie Components redirects to provider 3-D Secure authentication. The spec's test can use a challenge-required test card, while production issuer/frictionless and recurring merchant-initiated behavior may legitimately differ. Confirm whether FA-KTO-21 intends that test evidence or demands an interactive bank challenge for every charge. No universal force-challenge guarantee is established; test failed authentication and off-session reauthentication rather than assuming every renewal succeeds silently. [S3][M5][P7][F10]

## Invoice and organization preparation: concrete acceptance evidence

**Verified distinction:** Stripe explicitly says it creates invoice records but **does not itself generate/transmit compliant e-invoice files**. It names Billit as a preferred marketplace partner and documents a webhook integration extracting invoice/tax/customer data. This establishes a supported **integration route**, not that every app/country/profile supports EN 16931 or XRechnung. The selected supplier must name the applicable format, PO/BuyerReference mappings, delivery and rejection behavior, pricing, DPA and German customer coverage. Any account connection or sample-generation exercise belongs to the later authorized qualification work. [S4][S5]

Mollie's **Sales Invoices** is distinct from its older [Invoices API](https://docs.mollie.com/reference/invoices-api.md) for Mollie's own merchant-fee invoices. Sales Invoices provides native PDF, structured XML/e-invoicing and explicit `isEInvoice`, with DE/NL/BE sender/recipient restriction and Peppol registration. Documentation says an unregistered recipient can receive a standard email invoice instead; the API says a mismatched registered identifier fails delivery, so neither route can be called a universal structured-invoice guarantee. The inspected schema documents `memo` as appearing on the PDF and `metadata` as storage; no dedicated purchase-order field or proof that `memo` is emitted as the structured order reference was found. Its disclosed invoicing subprocessor is Storecove. This is promising **native product with underlying subprocessor**, not a reason to assume the public Storecove API is exposed to Mollie users. [M3][M4][M10]

Paddle proves bank-transfer/manual invoices and `billing_details.purchase_order_number`; FastSpring proves PO capture/payment terms and a separate Digital Invoicing product. **Neither inspected product/API documentation proves EN 16931 generation, validation or delivery.** Arbitrarily generating an operator-issued XRechnung for a MoR sale would risk naming the wrong seller or creating a second invoice; a compatible third-party solution must be accepted by the MoR and preserve the correct seller/credit-note/tax record. Until that evidence exists, both MoR candidates remain conditional on FA-ERW-06. [P2][F1][F2][P5][F7]

Official German guidance distinguishes structured EN 16931 from PDF, recognizes XRechnung and qualifying ZUGFeRD profiles, and permits multiple transmission methods (Peppol is not legally synonymous with e-invoicing). It describes domestic B2B scope, the €250 gross small-invoice exception, other exceptions and transitional issue periods through 2026/2027 depending on circumstances. The receipt obligation and public-sector requirements have their own scope; B2G may require routing identifiers and portals beyond a B2B invoice. Transitional relief does not defer **FA-ERW-06's launch selection evidence** or approve the future FA-KTO-25 acceptance test automatically. [L5]

**Required selection evidence / later acceptance exercise, not performed here:**

1. Name the exact product(s)/contracting entities and obtain an actual capability statement for a German operator, not an undated sales promise.
2. Describe a future organization purchase on invoice with payment terms, customer-supplied PO, transfer reconciliation, late/partial/overpayment handling and clear fulfillment-before/after-payment policy. A BNPL consumer “pay later” method alone does not prove this flow. [S10][M4][P2][F1]
3. Obtain a representative structured invoice over €250 to a German test business, correct seller/buyer identities/VAT treatment, PO in the correct semantic field, and any required BuyerReference/Leitweg-ID. Validate the **XML**, including current XRechnung rules required by FA-KTO-25, not a screenshot/PDF or “Peppol accepted” message. Retain validator version/results and a readable rendering. [L5; project FA-KTO-25/29]
4. Verify correction/credit-note, rejection/retry, archiving/export, recipient-without-Peppol and B2G delivery paths; define one authoritative legal invoice and ensure later account membership/owner changes do not rewrite the historical buyer. [S4][M3][L5; project FA-ERW-01/02]

## Payment, webhook and recovery requirements for the later model

**Verified provider behavior:** Stripe has API idempotency keys and signed webhooks; delivery may duplicate or arrive out of order. Mollie documents `Idempotency-Key` (especially important for duplicate recurring charges/subscriptions), payment webhooks and a newer signed-webhook system; its Recurring guide says **there are no subscription-status-specific webhooks** in that flow and retries can shift the next charge date. Paddle documents signed, at-least-once/out-of-order event delivery and event-ID deduplication. FastSpring documents HMAC-SHA256 webhook verification; automatic retries keep event IDs, but **manual retries create new event IDs**, so delivery-ID-only deduplication is insufficient for business effects. [S14][M11][M1][P10][F12]

**Proposed invariant set, to evaluate in the downstream billing/Quota decision; no ledger/schema is approved:**

- Bind checkout to a server-created purchase intent and immutable product/price/Quota/validity/consent version; validate all amounts/owner mappings server-side. A browser success redirect is UX, not payment proof. [S14][M11][P10][F12]
- Persist and verify incoming notifications before acknowledgement, queue durable processing, and deduplicate **both delivery/event identity and business effect** (e.g. one paid transaction/invoice grants one pack or period). Retrieve authoritative provider state where necessary; never grant Quota merely on invoice creation or pending bank-transfer/SEPA authorization. [S14][M4][P10][F3][F12]
- Use idempotency keys for outbound charge/subscription/refund APIs where supported; retain operation state across retries. Paddle/FastSpring API-wide idempotency behavior was not established here: do not assume Stripe-style keys. Use read-before-retry/provider IDs and reconciliation where needed. [S14][M11][P10][F12]
- Define separately “subscription active,” “invoice paid,” current paid-through date, scheduled cancellation and pack expiry. Plan changes, failed renewals, dunning, refunds/partial refunds and chargebacks need explicit entitlement/Quota policy, especially if already used. Cancellation stops future renewal while retaining paid access for the paid period; a pack remains a one-time dated purchase. [S9][M1][P3][F8; project FA-KTO-20/26/28]
- Define month boundary/time zone, retry-created billing-date movement, overlapping packs/Pro, spend order, upgrade/downgrade proration, expiry starting point (payment versus service activation), consent-deferred starts and refund effects. These are product decisions, not values to infer from vendor defaults. [M1; project FA-KTO-10/15/20/26]
- After database restore, reconcile provider transactions/subscriptions and replay missing notifications using business identifiers, preserve cancellation receipt/deletion/consent evidence and suppress duplicate grants/emails/charges. Provider retries are finite and cannot by themselves satisfy FA-UEB-45. External fiscal records and application Quota state must be reconciled before reopening paid actions. [S14][M11][P10][F12; sibling hosting research below]

Do not turn these recommendations into a selected billing model. The [sibling hosting findings](https://github.com/jeffnawroth/source-taster/blob/6886f0144d2edbe78072c31aaf9284e9cde14eea/docs/research/eu-managed-hosting.md) independently make restore/reconciliation and full location inventories necessary; they do not choose a payment provider or host.

### Two illustrative monthly baskets

Assume **100 Pro renewals at €12 + 100 one-time packs at €6**, all successful standard EEA consumer cards, EUR settlement, **€1,800 gross charged**, 200 transactions. Stripe illustration uses Billing, a paid invoice for each pack and Tax Basic low-code on every transaction. No refunds, FX, disputes, negotiated discounts or extra tax-provider charges. Direct processing and percentage bases are modeled on charged gross amounts; verify each executed tariff's base and tax treatment. A second basket is exactly ten times the quantities, **1,000 + 1,000** transactions. Arithmetic is derived from the cited list rates, not a vendor quotation. [S11][S12][M8]

| Monthly amount | Small: 100 Pro + 100 packs | Larger: 1,000 Pro + 1,000 packs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Gross customer charges | €1,800 | €18,000 |
| Stripe card processing: 1.5% gross + €0.25 each | €77.00 | €770.00 |
| Stripe Billing: 0.7% of Pro charges | €8.40 | €84.00 |
| Stripe Invoicing: 0.4% of pack charges | €2.40 | €24.00 |
| Stripe Tax Basic: 0.5% gross | €9.00 | €90.00 |
| **Illustrative Stripe subtotal** | **€96.80 (5.38%)** | **€968.00 (5.38%)** |
| **Mollie payment subtotal: 1.8% gross + €0.25 each** | **€82.40 (4.58%)** | **€824.00 (4.58%)** |

Stripe includes calculation software in that subtotal, Mollie does not include an equivalent verified multi-jurisdiction tax/filing stack in this model. Neither subtotal includes operator/accountant time or an external structured-invoice service. On these assumptions, Stripe fees per €12 Pro renewal are **€0.574**, per €6 pack **€0.394**; Mollie payments alone are **€0.466** / **€0.358**. Fixed fees make low-price packs expensive; batching purchases might reduce payment fees but must not alter product semantics without the client. Scaling this basket linearly leaves the percentage unchanged; real volume discounts and card/country mixes may change it. [S11][S12][M8]

**PayPal sensitivity:** let `p` be the actual contracted PayPal percentage (decimal) and `f` its EUR fixed fee; this research does not substitute an assumed PayPal plan. If the whole small basket used PayPal, Stripe's modeled total would be **`€43.40 + €1,800*p + 200*f`** (Stripe PayPal surcharge €23.60 + Billing €8.40 + pack invoicing €2.40 + Tax €9); Mollie payments **`€20 + €1,800*p + 200*f`**, before tax/accounting/invoicing extras. A mixed card/PayPal basket must use the actual shares and fees. Obtaining recurring PayPal approval and the underlying PayPal contract is therefore both a capability and cost gate. [S2][S11][M2][M8]

**MoR comparison without invented FX:** for a separate **US$12/US$6** basket of the same quantities, Paddle's checkout formula gives **US$190/month (10.56%)** on US$1,800, or **US$1,900** on the tenfold basket, using SRP as the modeled fee base under the supplier terms. This is **not an EUR-equivalent quote**. An all-EUR basket is `5% × applicable SRP + number of transactions × converted US$0.50`, subject to actual terms/FX/payout fee treatment. If buyer tax is added above sticker price rather than included, both charged total and fee base change; configure and quote on the same gross-price basis. A future €1,000-SRP Paddle invoice paid by bank transfer is illustratively €35 before other contract effects; card/PayPal settlement returns to checkout pricing. [P4][P6]

**Assessment of operator burden:** direct merchant processing gives more control over invoices and institutional procurement but leaves recurring tax/advisor/accounting, invoice corrections, customer notices, portal and payment-reconciliation responsibility. MoR reselling reduces buyer-tax administration and adds payment-related buyer support, at a larger visible commission and with seller/terms/data/migration constraints. For a solo operator, compare **total monthly cost = provider fees + accounting/tax/invoice services + support time + integration/recovery maintenance**, not just the payment percentage. The unknown value of the operator's time prevents a credible absolute “cheapest” ranking. [M9][P4][P6][F9][F13]

## Conditional shortlist and decision handoff

1. **Stripe direct + Billing + Invoicing + named structured-invoice integration** is the first path to qualify if the client accepts a combined service satisfying FA-ERW-06. Strong documented invoice payment/PO, portal, SCA, future SEPA and TypeScript primitives; tax calculation is configurable separately. Trade-offs are an additional structured-invoice supplier, PayPal recurring approval, retained direct-seller tax/legal work and unresolved global billing-data residency. Do not approve “Stripe alone” as EN 16931-capable. [S2][S4][S7][S8][S11][S13][T1]
2. **Mollie direct + Subscriptions + Sales Invoices** is the strongest alternative to qualify, especially if its native DE/NL/BE XML workflow and specific EU invoicing subcontractor evidence can reduce vendor count. The decisive questions are exact EN 16931/XRechnung output, PO semantic-field mapping, recipients without Peppol, external-bank reconciliation and full customer self-service/notification work. Its native structured invoicing must not be overlooked, but cannot be promoted to a complete FA-ERW-06 pass from the PDF memo alone. [M1][M3][M4][M8][M10][M11]
3. **Paddle Billing** is the lead MoR comparator for transparent checkout pricing and reduced buyer-tax operations. It documents cards/PayPal subscriptions/one-time purchases, bank-transfer invoices and PO fields. Hold selection until **EN 16931 evidence**, future SEPA preparation, German no-login cancellation authority/flow, consent evidence and payment-account storage scope are resolved. “MoR handles VAT” is valuable but does not satisfy those other requirements. [P1][P2][P3][P4][P6][P8][P10]
4. **FastSpring** is the MoR challenger when future SEPA and institutional invoice workflows weigh heavily. It documents both, but requires a numeric quote, precise invoice/manual-versus-auto-renewal design, **EN 16931 evidence**, German-buyer seller identification and acceptance of global data/contract terms. The inspected terms' low-volume fee and potential payout holds belong in the quote discussion. [F1][F3][F7][F9][F11][F13]

**Assessment:** there is no unconditional all-requirements winner in the evidence retrieved. That is a complete comparison result, not an unfinished vendor selection. First resolve the meaning of FA-ERW-06 and payment/account-data residency, then obtain the narrowly specified evidence for the preferred path. If a MoR cannot supply the required structured invoice, options are a genuinely supported correct-seller integration, another evidenced MoR, or a **client-approved versioned requirement change**; quietly waiting for organization launch is not an option under v3.1.
