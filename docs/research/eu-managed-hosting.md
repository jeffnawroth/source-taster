## 1. Reading the evidence and requirements

**Verified** means an official product document, public contract or first-party package/support record says it. A capability is not a contractual guarantee. **Assessment** identifies a research inference or recommendation for later decisions. **Unknown / procurement gate** identifies evidence still needed before purchase or launch. Links next to claims are the primary sources; calculations use the assumptions stated below.

The launch requirements relevant to hosting are:

- **FA-UEB-13:** account and verification data stored in the **EU**; outside-EU providers require GDPR-compliant safeguards. EU storage, EU ownership, EEA storage and EU-only processing are different properties. EU-only processing is deferred in **FA-UEB-14**, not a launch requirement. A DPA/SCC is not permission to disregard the EU storage clause.
- **FA-UEB-44:** measured application availability at least 99.5% per calendar month and planned maintenance announced 48 hours ahead. This is separate from the deferred organization contract in **FA-ORG-09**.
- **FA-UEB-45:** maximum lost data 24 hours and recovery within 8 hours for account, plan, billing and history data; recovery test before launch and annually. HA, backups, PITR and a successful recovery test answer different risks.
- **FA-UEB-10/11, FA-KTO-04:** deletion after a check without retained history, consent and history expiry, and account deletion must cover queues, logs, exports and recoveries as well as the live database. Billing retention is separately eight years. Provider defaults do not establish compliance.
- **FA-ERF-02/12:** PDF/DOCX input up to 20 MB and 1,000 References. **FA-SUC-07/08, FA-UEB-40–43:** concurrent Checks, progress, cancellation and the stated performance at 50 concurrent Checks. Pricing examples below do not prove those acceptance criteria.
- **FA-KTO-02/50, FA-UEB-01:** passwordless sign-in and transactional messages in German and English make mail delivery part of the launch dependency chain.
- **FA-ERF-09** (continue a Check after closing the window and notify on completion) remains **deferred**. An internal worker or queue can be an implementation choice for launch without promising that user-facing behavior. Durable workflow SaaS is not mandatory.

**Assessment:** first shortlist managed application runtimes and managed PostgreSQL; use object storage only for an identified purpose (e.g. encrypted independent database exports or retained verification reports). Original documents need not become permanent objects. Do not make an unapproved Node/Hono/pg-boss/SSE recommendation a hard hosting constraint.

## 2. Managed runtime candidates

### 2.1 Scalingo (France): managed PaaS plus PostgreSQL, external EU object storage/mail if needed

**Verified:** the documented public region `osc-fr1` is Paris on 3DS Outscale; the restricted SecNumCloud region is also Paris and requires support access. Do not rely on older references to a Strasbourg region. [Regions](https://doc.scalingo.com/platform/internals/regions).

**Verified:** the public SLA is 98% for one runtime container and 99.9% for two or more; databases are 98% Starter, 99.96% Business, 99.99% Enterprise. Scheduled maintenance, internet problems, customer software, force majeure and non-GA features are excluded. Credits, capped at 50%, are the stated remedy; claims must be made within three months. [Contractual SLA](https://scalingo.com/service-level-agreement).

**Assessment:** the cheapest one-container + Starter database combination does not even contractually cover the product's 99.5% target. Two web containers and a Business database are the first sensible production candidate, with separately redundant worker processes if workers are chosen. Even then, the host SLA does not prove Source Taster availability, and counting a web process plus a worker as “two containers” needs explicit contract confirmation.

**Verified:** on PostgreSQL Shared Resources, managed logical backups and PITR are included. Shared Starter and Business PITR windows are seven days. Backups are encrypted, duplicated across three availability zones **in the database's region**; Business logical retention includes seven daily, eight weekly and twelve monthly points. The initial PITR base backup can take up to 24 hours; deleting a database removes continuous backups, while logical backups expire separately. PITR resets the timeline. Backup load/locks/maintenance can delay or skip logical backups; provider events identify backup failures and continuous backup delay/staleness. Dedicated Resources have different retention and customer-managed logical exports: do not transfer Shared assumptions to them. [Backup policies, updated 2026-10-14](https://doc.scalingo.com/databases/about/backup-policies).

**Assessment:** PITR offers a credible path to RPO 24h, not an 8h application restore guarantee. Independent encrypted exports, backup-age alerts, a tested connection switch, replay-safe billing integration, and a deletion-aware restore procedure remain operator work. Same-region multi-AZ backups are not independent protection from account compromise or a region-wide incident.

**Verified:** the Scalingo DPA's Article 8 specifies French Outscale data centers and French OOS storage for database and log backups; Article 13 names Outscale as the hosted-database subprocessor, with no EU transfer for that listed purpose. This is stronger evidence than a region selector alone. Its controller activities for the operator's own platform account/support are separately described; that is not permission to put Source Taster customer content in support tickets. [DPA](https://scalingo.com/data-processing-agreement). **Unknown / procurement gate:** archive the executed DPA and current subprocessor register, verify support access locations and any additional integrations; don't interpret the short hosting list as a universal “no third parties” claim.

**Verified:** Scalingo supports separate Procfile process types, including worker processes; routing supports SSE/WebSockets, HTTP/2 at the public proxy and HTTP/1.1 to the container. The app must send the first response data within 30 seconds, then send data at least every 59 seconds to avoid a 504. [Procfile](https://doc.scalingo.com/platform/app/procfile), [routing](https://doc.scalingo.com/platform/networking/public/routing). **Verified:** Scalingo ingress limits request bodies to 75 MB, above the required 20 MB input size; the application must still enforce its own 20 MB limit and safely parse untrusted PDFs/DOCX. [Ingress limits](https://doc.scalingo.com/platform/networking/public/ingress). **Assessment:** SSE with short heartbeats and reconnectable progress is compatible in principle; validate actual response flushing, cancellation and deployment interruption. “HTTP/2 supported” does not require Node to speak HTTP/2 internally.

**Verified:** included Basic support is business-hours support; paid support starts at the greater of €100/month or 5% usage for Starter, €500 or 10% for Business. The vendor describes response/intervention commitments, not application recovery guarantees. [Support plans](https://scalingo.com/support-plans). **Assessment:** ordinary support cannot substitute for an automated recovery path during operator sleep/absence.

### 2.2 Render (Frankfurt): convenient PaaS, EU runtime with outside-EU provider safeguards

**Verified:** Frankfurt, Germany is a supported service region; Render offers web services, continuously running background workers, managed PostgreSQL, cron, Docker and static sites. Keep API, worker and database in Frankfurt for the private network. These are capabilities, not a selection of any framework. [Regions](https://render.com/docs/regions), [workers](https://render.com/docs/background-workers), [web services](https://render.com/docs/web-services), [pricing](https://render.com/pricing).

**Verified:** paid PostgreSQL includes continuous PITR; Hobby workspace recovery window is three days, Pro and above seven days. A restore creates a **new instance**: the customer validates it and switches connection strings. The chosen restore timestamp must be at least ten minutes old. Logical exports can be downloaded and are retained seven days; free database compute has no managed recovery. [Recovery and backups](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-backups).

**Verified:** HA requires PostgreSQL 13+ and at least one CPU. The standby is in another zone of the same region, billed at the same compute/storage rate as the primary. Replication is asynchronous: failover can lose recent writes. Automatic failover starts after 30 seconds of primary unavailability and the connection URL stays the same; clients must reconnect. HA does not protect against both zones failing or customer deletion. [HA documentation](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-high-availability).

**Verified:** the pricing page places contractual uptime SLAs in **Enterprise (custom pricing)**. Pro's $25/month workspace fee plus paid compute does **not** buy a public contractual uptime percentage. [Pricing](https://render.com/pricing). **Assessment:** a production deployment may meet 99.5% through its measured behavior without an upstream contractual SLA; whether that commercial risk is acceptable is a client choice, not a requirements relaxation. HA and multiple web/worker instances can reduce specific failure risks but cannot prove the application SLO.

**Verified:** Render's DPA allows international processing and includes DPF/EU SCC transfer mechanisms (sections 4 and 6). Its security page lists AWS, GCP, Cloudflare and ClickHouse Inc., each with entity country United States. Entity country is not the physical location of every workload. [DPA](https://render.com/dpa), [current subprocessor list](https://render.com/security). **Unknown / procurement gate:** the public region and DPA evidence read here does not establish EU storage for **all** service logs, database backup copies, control-plane customer metadata or support attachments. Obtain a written data-location matrix for the selected products. Until then classify Render as **conditional under FA-UEB-13**, not verified compliant just because Frankfurt exists. Disable global caching of authenticated/content responses and keep customer content out of platform support.

**Unknown / acceptance gate:** the retrieved evidence does not establish all proxy/upload-body and SSE-flushing limits for the selected Render service; verify 20 MB upload, long-running progress/reconnect and cancellation through the real ingress. **Assessment:** operationally attractive for a solo operator, but additional processors for object storage/mail/monitoring and the unresolved residency matrix can outweigh the cheap entry price. Outside-EU ownership is not itself forbidden by FA-UEB-13; neither SCCs nor DPF alone establish its EU-at-rest storage requirement.

### 2.3 Scaleway (EU services): containers/jobs + managed PostgreSQL + Object Storage + TEM + Cockpit

**Verified:** Serverless Containers accept `linux/amd64` container images, HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2, maximum per-instance request concurrency 80, and configurable HTTP request durations from ten seconds to **60 minutes**. Scaling to zero and cold starts are possible; minimum scale can mitigate them. Dedicated outgoing IPs are not provided. Jobs run up to **24 hours**, are not an indefinitely running worker, and also require amd64 images. [Container limitations](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/serverless-containers/reference-content/containers-limitations/), [Jobs limitations](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/serverless-jobs/reference-content/jobs-limitations/), [container pricing](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/serverless/).

**Assessment:** this is an alternative execution model, not a drop-in guarantee for the sibling research's always-running PostgreSQL queue worker. Request-driven execution, minimum-scale CPU behavior and job scheduling must be designed together. Do not start a never-ending poller and assume the serverless lifecycle preserves it. Cold-start and progress-flushing tests are material to the ten-second first-verdict limit. The retrieved limitations do not establish a full 20 MB upload-body path; confirm proxy/body limits and test it, together with memory use during PDF/DOCX extraction. No dedicated egress IP can also matter for Data-source allowlisting. Runtime and database should use the same EU region/private connectivity where supported; allow for gateway/network costs if required by the final topology.

**Verified:** the Container SLA is **99.5%**, and counts a five-minute interval unavailable only when at least twenty valid requests were made and more than half return qualifying 500/503 errors unrelated to application code. This differs from product availability, especially at low traffic. The Managed Database SLA covers PostgreSQL/MySQL **POP2 nodes only**, with 99.5% standalone and 99.95% HA; other node families shown on the price page are expressly excluded. Scheduled maintenance and other exclusions apply. [Container SLA](https://www.scaleway.com/en/serverless-containers/sla/), [database SLA, last modified 2025-06-26](https://www.scaleway.com/en/cloud-database-solutions/sla/). **Assessment:** a low DB-DEV/PLAY price must never be presented as purchasing the POP2 SLA. Even a POP2 HA database does not create application-level availability headroom above a 99.5% runtime commitment.

**Verified:** database autobackup is daily with seven-day retention by default; Local Storage uses backups and Block Storage uses **snapshots**, with different portability. Snapshots stay in the original location; logical backups can target another region through the API. Large backup restores can take hours. Encryption at rest must be enabled for database volumes; the concepts page explicitly says logical backup encryption is not currently available. [Manage backups](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/managed-databases-for-postgresql-and-mysql/how-to/manage-backups/), [concepts](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/managed-databases-for-postgresql-and-mysql/concepts/). **Unknown:** no managed PITR for this specific PostgreSQL product was established from these sources; do not conflate serverless SQL, snapshots or another product's SLA with it. **Assessment:** a schedule more frequent than 24h plus monitored successful snapshots and independently encrypted exports is the prudent RPO path; exactly one backup per day has no failure margin.

**Verified:** the [DPA dated 2024-06-01](https://www-uploads.scaleway.com/DPA_2024_ENG_b0abb5cc26.pdf), linked from [contracts](https://www.scaleway.com/en/contracts/), says services are located in the EU by default; the client selects region where offered (Article 11). It permits informed outside-EU transfers with safeguards, not an unconditional EU-only promise. Article 7 permits subprocessors and gives thirty days' notice of changes to subscribers. **Procurement gate:** capture the current service-specific subprocessor list and processing/access countries; confirm selected-region backup, mail and observability coverage in the agreement. “Sovereign” marketing does not replace that inventory.

**Verified:** Basic support's P1 initial-response time is eight hours; Advanced is €50/month or 5% net spend (greater), with two-hour P1 response; Business €250 or 10% with thirty-minute P1 response. These are **initial responses**, not restores. [Support](https://www.scaleway.com/en/support/). **Assessment:** waiting the full Basic response time already exhausts FA-UEB-45's recovery budget. Automated self-service recovery or an independently operable fallback is needed regardless of support plan.

**Additional verified subprocessor evidence:** Scaleway's [public list, reviewed July 2025](https://www.scaleway.com/en/subprocessorlist/) names EU processing locations for colocation, including Equinix, Iron Mountain and Digital Realty despite their US headquarters. Atempo is listed for Object Storage cold-storage recording/archiving; other entries are product-specific (e.g. Clever Cloud for Web Platform). This is not evidence that every listed company receives every customer's data. The dated list needs reconfirmation for the actual services selected.

### 2.4 Clever Cloud: credible EU PaaS alternative, request a concrete production quote

**Verified:** managed PostgreSQL supports stable 18 (18.6 in the retrieved table), daily backups with seven-day retention by default, optional replication, and PITR through pgBackRest **on request**, not installed by default. DEV databases have no backups or SLA and are for testing. Cellar is an S3-compatible object storage offering. [PostgreSQL](https://www.clever.cloud/developers/doc/deploy/databases/postgresql/), [Cellar](https://www.clever.cloud/product/cellar-object-storage/), [runtime documentation](https://www.clever.cloud/developers/doc/deploy/applications/nodejs/).

**Verified:** general terms §5 promise **99.9% per year**, with exclusions including maintenance scheduled ten days ahead; do not misquote that as a monthly product SLA. The [DPA](https://www.clever.cloud/general-terms-and-conditions-of-use/clever-cloud-data-processing-agreement/) points to a [subprocessor list v1.6, 2026-09-01](https://cdn.clever-cloud.com/uploads/2026/09/clever-clouds-sub-processors.pdf). It identifies French Paris infrastructure options (Green Data, Telehouse, nLighten, Etix, Scaleway), also non-EU regions, and US locations for Twilio support/Pipedrive CRM. The document says customers choose the hosting data center. [Terms](https://www.clever.cloud/general-terms-and-conditions-of-use/).

**Assessment / procurement gate:** keep Clever Cloud as an alternative to Scalingo if its combined PaaS/Cellar offer and quote are better. Obtain a complete selected-region quote including redundant API/worker instances, database replication and PITR window, backup location, access countries and any monthly SLA option. The retrieved [public pricing page](https://www.clever.cloud/pricing/) did not expose a reproducible complete configuration price in its text; no invented “from” total is supplied here. The annual SLA, default daily dump and unspecified PITR activation are substantive differences to resolve before comparing it to the priced candidates.

## 3. Object storage, email, monitoring and execution choices

### 3.1 Object storage: optional product storage, necessary independent backup destination if that strategy is selected

**Verified:** Scaleway Standard Multi-AZ in Paris is €0.01606/GB/month, Standard One Zone €0.00803, with requests/ingress included; egress includes 75 GB/month then €0.01/GB (documented intra/inter-regional transfers have exemptions). Glacier is cheaper but has restoration costs. Standard Multi-AZ, One Zone and Glacier are different durability/availability/recovery choices. [Price table](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/storage/), [Object Storage](https://www.scaleway.com/en/object-storage/), [service-specific SLA](https://www.scaleway.com/en/object-storage/sla/).

**Assessment:** Standard Multi-AZ in an explicitly selected EU region is the low-cost default candidate for independent encrypted exports and any retained verification reports; do not use cold storage as the sole eight-hour recovery route. “Multi-AZ” does not mean cross-provider protection. An export from Scalingo/Render to Scaleway reduces provider dependency; an export from Scaleway DB to the same Scaleway account does not. Use separate access/retention controls and keep decryption keys recoverable outside the failed runtime. Cellar is another candidate where a Clever Cloud quote is preferred. The S3 protocol itself is not a data-location guarantee.

**Verified:** [lifecycle rules](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/object-storage/how-to/manage-lifecycle-rules/) support automated object management. **Assessment:** choose retention separately for transient input, retained history/report data and recovery exports. Explicitly handle older versions, delete markers, incomplete multipart uploads, failed task artifacts and cached downloads; a normal delete of the current object is not proof that all copies are gone. Avoid putting non-consented source documents in an immutable backup bucket. A 24-hour lifecycle timer is not evidence of physical deletion by the exact FA-UEB-10 deadline: the end-of-Check timestamp and actual purge behavior need acceptance evidence.

### 3.2 Transactional email: EU storage is possible; delivery and recipient geography remain separate

**Verified – Scaleway TEM:** available only in `fr-par`; SMTP/REST API, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, delivery events/blocklists, Essential pay-as-you-go and Scale. Essential includes 300 emails per organization then €0.25 per 1,000; Scale €80/month includes 100,000 then €0.20 per 1,000 and a 99.9% SLA. Essential has **no SLA**. Default quota is 10,000/month and increases require validation; each recipient counts separately. API message size is 2 MB, SMTP default 50 MB. [Capabilities/limits](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/transactional-email/reference-content/tem-capabilities-and-limits/), [pricing](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/managed-services/#transactional-email), [product](https://www.scaleway.com/en/transactional-email-tem/), [specific terms dated 2026-04-07](https://www-uploads.scaleway.com/Specific_Conditions_Transactionnal_Email_Services_v042026_3f5487a93c.pdf).

**Verified – MailPace alternative:** its [DPA, updated 2026-10-08](https://mailpace.com/dpa) says stored transactional email data is encrypted and hosted in **France**, but mail is sent through **UK SMTP servers**, then to recipients' servers worldwide. Its [privacy notice](https://mailpace.com/privacy) identifies the legal entity as OhMySMTP Ltd, England and Wales; “European company” is not “EU legal entity.” The DPA's default retention is thirty days in the dashboard plus seven days in backups, with subprocessor identities available on request. The [pricing page](https://mailpace.com/pricing) advertises API/SMTP, idempotency and customizable retention, but its currency-dependent numeric quote was not exposed in retrieved text; confirm a quote rather than inventing a price or SLA.

**Assessment:** TEM is the most directly evidenced low-cost email companion to the priced combinations; MailPace is a reasonable second option if UK processing, retention and its complete subprocessor chain are accepted. EU-only processing would need a separate decision if FA-UEB-14 is activated. Even an EU sender cannot promise the recipient's mailbox is in the EU. Send sign-in codes/links, billing and cancellation messages with minimal account metadata; do not email References, original documents or report attachments by default. German/English templates are application responsibility. Test delivery to common providers, authentication/DNS, abuse limits, bounce handling, expiry, idempotency and retry behavior. A provider's API uptime does not guarantee inbox placement. A fallback mail provider is a deliberate extra processor/cost, not a default requirement.

### 3.3 Monitoring: use EU telemetry and an independent content-free external view

**Verified – Cockpit:** metrics/logs from integrated Scaleway products stay in the originating region; custom sources use a selected region and are not replicated to other regions. It can ingest data from outside Scaleway. Native data is included; custom logs/traces €0.35/GB, custom metrics starting €0.15/million samples; default retention seven days logs/traces and 31 days metrics; each active alert rule €0.015/day. Export/extended retention is extra. [Location FAQ](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/cockpit/faq/), [pricing](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/managed-services/#cockpit).

**Verified – Sentry alternative:** selecting EU stores event data and its backups in Frankfurt, but user accounts, organization settings, tokens, cron check-ins and support interactions may remain in the US; uptime checks can be stored in all locations. The storage region cannot be changed in-place. [Detailed location matrix](https://docs.sentry.io/organization/data-storage-location/). **Assessment:** useful error diagnostics if payloads are scrubbed and outside-EU metadata is accepted, but not a blanket EU solution. Disable session replay/content capture and avoid sending end-user email, tokens, document text or References. No Sentry cost is hidden in the scenarios below.

**Verified – independent external monitoring candidate:** Oh Dear's Solo plan is €15/month for two sites/servers and includes monitoring/status-page features. Its [subprocessors](https://ohdear.app/subprocessors) include Combell Belgium for main storage, ClickHouse EU, AWS/global for probes and backup/report storage, DigitalOcean/Vultr global probes and Cloudflare notifications. Its [DPA](https://ohdear.app/data-processing-agreement) and [EU residency page](https://ohdear.app/features/gdpr) must be read with that list. [Pricing](https://ohdear.app/pricing).

**Assessment:** use only public, synthetic health/readiness endpoints with no customer identifiers, query parameters containing secrets or content. That provides a view independent from the compute provider without exporting verification data. Restrict monitoring scope rather than infer every globally replicated monitor record satisfies EU account/verification storage. Do not configure an automatic site crawl over authenticated content. External probes alone cannot measure sign-in, billing and the whole Check path: add internal synthetic application transactions with test data and record separate Data-source degradation. Alert through a channel that does not share every dependency with the product.

### 3.4 Internal execution and queues

**Verified:** pg-boss 12.38.0 is MIT, requires Node ≥22.12 and PostgreSQL ≥13, and documents retries, transactional job creation, polling/LISTEN/NOTIFY and scheduling. [First-party README](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss), [publisher metadata](https://registry.npmjs.org/pg-boss/12.38.0). **Assessment:** if selected later, a queue in managed PostgreSQL avoids another hosted data store and processor. Store identifiers in job payloads, not document text. Account for schema permissions, pooling, LISTEN/NOTIFY connections, concurrency/backpressure, retry retention, idempotency and cancellation. The package's transactional/queue semantics cannot guarantee exactly-once external email or payment side effects.

**Verified:** Scaleway Queues provides an SQS-compatible service in Paris/Amsterdam; the pricing table uses €0.01/GB with a 256 KB message limit. Queues are execution coordination, not worker compute. [Product](https://www.scaleway.com/en/queues/), [pricing](https://www.scaleway.com/en/pricing/managed-databases/#messaging-and-queuing), [FAQ](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/queues/faq/). **Assessment:** it can support stateless container/Jobs execution, but use Check IDs and pointers and design visibility timeout, redelivery, poison-message handling and deletion. Don't transport 20 MB inputs in messages. A Redis-based queue is another architecture with another stateful dependency and backup/security task; none is required by the specification.

**Verified:** [Temporal Cloud's region list](https://docs.temporal.io/evaluate/cloud/regions) includes AWS Frankfurt/Ireland. The retrieved page warns that new GCP Frankfurt namespace provisioning is capacity-restricted; listed product availability is not a promise of immediate account capacity. **Unknown:** this pass does not establish a complete data-location/subprocessor/cost case for Temporal Cloud, Trigger.dev Cloud or Inngest Cloud; an EU region is insufficient evidence. **Assessment:** do not introduce one solely to satisfy deferred FA-ERF-09. If a future architecture prefers durable workflows, perform that service-specific evaluation then; it is not a hidden dependency of the costed launch candidates.

**Assessment:** use a patched LTS Node release as the conservative launch candidate; reconsider 26 after its actual LTS transition and deployment/compatibility verification. Service API version labels are not package semver; verify GA/support for any chosen cloud product rather than assuming a `v1beta*` endpoint proves or disproves product maturity. ## 6. Availability and recovery: what remains the operator's responsibility

### 6.1 Contract availability and product availability

**Calculation:** 99.5% permits 0.5% of a month: **216 minutes (3h36m)** in 30 days, 223.2 minutes in 31 days, 201.6 minutes in 28 days. One eight-hour outage in a 30-day month produces about **98.89%**, even if it meets the separate eight-hour recovery limit. An eight-hour RTO is therefore a disaster ceiling, not an acceptable routine incident duration under FA-UEB-44. Both requirements remain in force.

**Assessment:** an application can fail when the runtime, database, worker, DNS, authentication email or critical code path fails. Upstream SLAs describe different windows, exclusions, error definitions and credit remedies; multiplying their percentages does not certify the product. Scalingo's two-container class and Business database, Render HA, or Scaleway POP2 HA are **risk controls**, not an accepted monthly availability result. Same-zone redundancy, same-region backups, shared credentials and shared-provider monitoring leave correlated failure risks.

For #22, define the measured surface and publish monthly evidence: public app/API readiness, sign-in, start/progress/cancel a synthetic Check, quota/account consistency and ordinary purchase/cancellation paths with provider-safe tests. Distinguish a running service that returns a permitted “Not verifiable” after all Data sources fail from a service outage; **FA-SUC-05** defines the former behavior, but the product availability measurement needs a client-approved boundary. Use synthetic References and separate Data-source reachability metrics. Count actual user-visible failures, including application bugs and planned maintenance, unless the client explicitly clarifies otherwise; don't import a vendor's exclusions silently.

**48-hour maintenance gate:** none of the retrieved sources establishes that every infrastructure security/failover event will arrive with 48 hours' notice to Source Taster. Scalingo/Scaleway contractual maintenance exclusions are not evidence of notice compliance; Clever's general terms explicitly describe scheduled maintenance with ten days' notice, but propagation into the product remains operator work. Track vendor notices, announce operator-planned maintenance ≥48h ahead, use redundant/rolling deployment where supported and test updates. Ask the client how emergency security work and upstream unannounced maintenance should be treated; don't promise notice the operator cannot control.

### 6.2 RPO 24h: credible paths and failure margins

**Assessment:** Scalingo Shared PITR and Render paid PITR provide the clearest documented path to a recovery point much more recent than 24h. Scaleway's documented daily snapshot/backup path can also be designed toward the requirement, but **one scheduled backup every 24h is insufficient evidence**: delay, one failed run or backup duration can put the newest recoverable point beyond 24h. Configure more frequent successful recovery points and alert on age. No host's defaults prove the acceptance test.

A candidate policy to validate in #22:

1. Enable managed recovery before accepting production data; verify PITR initialization and a recent recoverable point rather than merely a “backup enabled” toggle.
2. Export the recovery-critical account/plan/billing/history database at least every six hours to encrypted EU storage under separate credentials, in addition to managed PITR/snapshots. Scope payload tables and deletion behavior deliberately; don't export transient documents automatically. Six hours is a proposed operating margin, not a requirements change or an already adopted schedule.
3. Alert on backup failure and excessive latest-success/recoverable-point age early enough to fix it **before** 24h; test alerts and ensure logs don't expose keys/content. Scalingo's documented continuous-backup events mark >12h delayed and >24h stale, so the stale event alone is too late. [Backup policy](https://doc.scalingo.com/databases/about/backup-policies).
4. Preserve tested migration artifacts, deployable source/build images, configuration and encrypted secret recovery separately. Database backups do not recreate DNS, email domain configuration, provider credentials or an expired payment webhook secret.
5. Check object/report recoverability and database/object consistency; regenerating a report is acceptable only if the required retained evidence and scoring-rules version remain recoverable. Account, plan, billing and consent records cannot be reconstructed from a report alone.

### 6.3 RTO 8h: feasible only with a measured, independent restore procedure

**Verified restore differences:**

- **Scalingo Shared:** PITR is an in-place, database-stopping **dashboard-only** restore that cannot be cancelled/undone and resets its timeline. Logical restoration is command-line `pg_restore`, also with downtime. The dashboard-only path is an automation/absence consideration. [Restore guide](https://doc.scalingo.com/databases/postgresql/shared-resources/guides/restoring).
- **Render:** PITR creates another instance; the customer validates data and reconnects services, which permits isolated validation but needs sufficient account quota/capacity and correct secret rotation. [Restore guide](https://render.com/docs/postgresql-backups).
- **Scaleway:** backups can restore into the original or another database, with large restores taking hours. Snapshots/local backups have different location and portability constraints. [Manage backups](https://www.scaleway.com/en/docs/managed-databases-for-postgresql-and-mysql/how-to/manage-backups/).

**Assessment:** eight hours is plausible for a small database with accessible credentials, available restore capacity and an exercised runbook. It is **not verified** by any vendor page in this research, and not credible if first action depends on a sleeping solo operator or an eight-hour initial support response. Paid support helps escalation; it does not replace recovery execution or promise repair. No account was provisioned and no restore/load test was run here.

For the launch recovery test and annual repeat required by FA-UEB-45, #22 needs evidence for at least accidental deletion/corruption and loss of the primary service; also state whether full provider/account/region loss is in the disaster scenario. A proposed exercise uses synthetic data at a realistic retained size and:

- starts the stopwatch at service disruption, including detection, operator mobilisation and vendor escalation;
- obtains a known recent point from managed recovery and separately tests the independently exported backup;
- provisions an EU target, restores compatible versions/extensions/roles, deploys exact app artifacts and recovers secrets without relying on the broken host;
- applies deletion tombstones and expiry/consent rules **before** reopening access, so restoration does not resurrect deleted accounts/history;
- reconnects API/worker, verifies sign-in, account/plan/quota/billing/history and payment/webhook idempotency, cancels/reconciles interrupted Checks and suppresses duplicate email or charges;
- records recovered-point age, achieved full application recovery time and remaining manual steps, then tears down test data/resources.

A **hypothetical target budget**, to measure rather than assert: detection 10m + mobilisation 20m + provisioning 20m + restore 60m + validation 30m + cutover 10m + reconciliation 20m = **170 minutes**. That fits inside one 30-day availability budget with little room for another incident; it also leaves disaster margin below eight hours. Failure to achieve it is evidence for more redundancy/automation or client clarification, not permission to mark FA-UEB-44/45 met. A real 80 GB restore may be much slower than the 20 GB illustration. Someone with authority and credentials must be available to recover when agents/providers fail; agree on coverage and escalation costs with the client.

**No requirement is changed or waived by this research.** These questions go to the client through #22 / #21 / #26 and the parent map; they are not new approved requirements or automatically necessary specification changes.

### Candidate requirement clarifications (and only if necessary later, versioned change requests)
